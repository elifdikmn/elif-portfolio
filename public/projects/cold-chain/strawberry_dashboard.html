1. The same shipment looks very different depending on where you measure

Each line is one logger, measured temperature (not a model). Values are 10-minute averages; gaps of up to 20 minutes are filled with the last value. Red band = confirmed excursion (a logger at or above 39.2 °F for 30+ minutes). Click a logger to show or hide it.

Top loggers were generally the warmest, while Bottom loggers stayed cooler, so a sensor in the wrong spot could miss a warm period. Not every Top logger is warmest at every moment.

3. Position matters more than count

Range of hot-time coverage over every possible set of k loggers. Box = middle half, dot = median.

Three well-placed loggers (98%) beat five poorly placed ones. Three loggers can also see almost nothing (0%), depending on where they are.

This is specific to these four event-bearing shipments (where the top of the pallet warmed first). It is not a universal placement rule.

4. Early warning: what a simple temperature alarm can do

For each of the 8 excursion events: was there a fresh alarm in the 2 hours before it started?

A low threshold "covers" everything only by staying on almost all the time. Only a fresh alarm counts as an early warning, and at 39.0 °F that is 3 of 8 events, with a median lead of about 30 minutes.

5. Forecasting the peak temperature

Forecast error (shipment-macro RMSE, lower is better), tested on shipments the model had not seen.

The model helps modestly. The simple baseline is already good, and sudden jumps of +1.5 to +2.4 °F within 30 minutes did not show a reliable signal beforehand. The differences between methods are much smaller than the loggers' nominal accuracy (±1.8 °F), so their practical importance is uncertain.

What this does and does not show

  • Shows: in these data, seeing the pallet from several well-chosen points reveals hot time that a single or badly placed sensor misses.
  • Does not show: that any specific smart-pallet product performs better. All sensors here measure temperature only; humidity, GPS or ambient sensors were not available.
  • Small evidence base: 6 shipments, 8 excursion events, only 4 shipments with events. Results are descriptive and need testing on new shipments.
